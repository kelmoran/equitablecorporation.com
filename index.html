The Equitable Corporation

Equitable Corporation

Type: Corporate structure

Standard: Equitable Standard

Founder: Ethan Wei, MD

What it is

An Equitable Corporation is a company built on a simple belief: the people who create the value should keep it. Much of today's tech is built on extraction — your data, attention, and effort are used to generate value that flows away from you. The Equitable Corporation inverts that: it is structured so the value created on the platform is not extracted, and the core principles that protect users are enforced in three layers — the cryptographic durability of the signed record, a charter-level bar on capture and on weakening the terms, and a per-signee remedy — so they cannot be quietly revised away.

The core commitments

Every Equitable Corporation commits to the same locked set of principles. In short: user data stays owned by the user and is never sold, mined, or read. The charter requires a supermajority and a right of first refusal for any sale of control, the core IP, or the brand to a private equity firm, a venture capital fund, or any acquirer that would control the board — so a capture is priced and public, not possible to do quietly. Permitted acquirers are limited to a mission-aligned for-profit research institution that adopts the principles in writing, a founder- and staff-directed buyout, or a dissolution into a non-profit or research-purpose trust. The platform is user-auditable — every line of deployed code is auditable by your IT security team — and the signed record verifies independently of the company's ownership.

How we are paid

The founder owns 100% of the equity at inception and takes no outside capital. The founder is paid a board-approved salary capped at a published benchmark, and receives distributions of distributable profits per a locked ratio. The 1% certification fee is revenue from other companies adopting the Standard — it is not the founder's pay.

How it's enforced

How it's enforced

The principles are enforced in three layers. The signed record is cryptographically durable — it verifies against the public chain (or your own key), independent of the issuer. A charter-level bar — supermajority + ROFR on any sale of control, the core IP, or the brand to a for-profit acquirer, and on any material weakening of the terms. And a per-signee remedy that a capture or an adverse change breaches. No big upfront legal build-out is required. The full enforcement structure.

Why a corporation, not a non-profit

The structure needs real upkeep, legal standing, and earnings to stay afloat against other corporations. A non-profit would cap the founder's earning potential and can't compete the way a for-profit can. The Equitable Corporation lets a company sustain itself and compete — while its core commitments are enforced in three layers (the record's cryptographic durability, the charter-level bar, and the per-signee remedy), which only ever get stronger.
