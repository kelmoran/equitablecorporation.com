Frequently Asked Questions

What is the Equitable Corporation?

A corporate structure in which the value created by users is not extracted: user data stays owned by the user, the company cannot be acquired, and the core rules are locked so they cannot be changed later.

How is it different from a normal corporation?

In a normal corporation, user data and attention are assets that can be sold, and the company can be sold to an acquirer. In an Equitable Corporation, user data stays owned by the user, the company cannot be acquired by private equity or large firms, and the governing rules are locked at inception.

Is the commitment permanent?

Yes. The core commitments are locked at inception and cannot be altered. A company can add features and grow, but it cannot change the rules that protect its users. Once certified under the standard, a company cannot opt out.

How is transparency handled?

The commitments are verifiable rather than merely promised: governance, financials, and data practices are open to audit. What stays private is the user's own work and data — those are never the company's to disclose or use.

Can any company adopt this structure?

Yes. The Equitable Standard is open to any incorporated entity that genuinely commits to the principles. Applying is free, and certification takes about 30 days.
