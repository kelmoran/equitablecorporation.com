Frequently Asked Questions

What is the Equitable Corporation?

A corporate structure in which the value created by users is not extracted: user data stays owned by the user, the charter requires a supermajority and a right of first refusal for any sale of control, the core IP, or the brand to a private equity firm, a venture capital fund, or any acquirer that would control the board, and the signed record verifies independently of the company's ownership.

How is it different from a normal corporation?

In a normal corporation, user data and attention are assets that can be sold, and the company can be sold to an acquirer. In an Equitable Corporation, user data stays owned by the user, and the charter requires a supermajority and a right of first refusal for any sale or change of control to a private equity firm, a venture capital fund, or any acquirer that would control the board — so a capture is priced and public. Permitted acquirers are limited to a mission-aligned for-profit research institution that adopts the principles in writing, a founder- and staff-directed buyout, or a dissolution into a non-profit or research-purpose trust. The signed record verifies independently of the company's ownership, so it outlasts any single owner.

Is the commitment permanent?

Yes. The core terms can only be strengthened, never weakened — a material adverse change to them (weakening data ownership, enabling sale/mining of the signee's data or records, or reducing auditability) requires the stated supermajority and triggers the per-signee remedy. A company can add features and grow, but it cannot walk back the principles that protect its users.

How is transparency handled?

The commitments are verifiable rather than merely promised: every line of deployed code is auditable by your IT security team, and governance, financials, and data practices are open to audit. What stays private is the user's own work and data — those are never the company's to disclose or use. And the signed record is cryptographically durable — it verifies against the public chain (or your own key), independent of the company's future ownership or survival. So the protection is three layers: the record outlives us; the charter makes capture costly and public; and a capture or an adverse change breaches the agreement with every signee.

Can any company adopt this structure?

Yes. The Equitable Standard is open to any incorporated entity that genuinely commits to the principles. Applying is free, and certification takes about 30 days.
